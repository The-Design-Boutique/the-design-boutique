Setting Up Email

So the website can send you form enquiries  |  The Design Boutique  |  July 2026

A website cannot send email on its own. It has to hand each message to a service built for the job, and that service has to be given permission to send using your domain name. Without that permission, anything it sent would land in spam folders, because email providers have no way of telling it apart from somebody pretending to be you.

The service we use is called Resend. This guide sets it up. It is a one time job.

Nothing is broken in the meantime

Until this is done, forms on the site still work and every submission is still saved. You will find them in the editor under Form Submissions. What is missing is only the email telling you a new one has arrived. Nothing is lost.

You do not have to do step 3 yourself

Steps 1, 2 and 4 take about ten minutes and need no technical knowledge. Step 3 involves your domain settings, which is the one part where a mistake can affect your existing email. Angelo or Robert will do that part for you. Send them the screen Resend shows you and they will take it from there.

Step 1: Create the Resend account

1Sign up

https://resend.com/signup

Use a work email address you will keep access to. The free plan sends 3,000 emails a month, which is far more than a contact form will ever need. There is no card required.

Step 2: Tell Resend about your domain

2Add the domain

https://resend.com/domains

Click Add Domain. When it asks for the name, we recommend entering:

send.thedesignboutique.com

Note the send. at the front. That is deliberate and worth understanding: it keeps website emails separate from the email you and your team send day to day. If the website ever sends something that upsets a spam filter, it cannot damage the reputation of your ordinary email. It also means the setup in step 3 cannot disturb the email you already use.

Step 3: Prove the domain is yours

Resend will now show you a table of settings to add to your domain. This is how you prove you own it: only somebody with control of the domain could add them, so the settings are the proof.

These live wherever your domain is managed, which is usually the company you bought it from. The screen will look something like this, with three or four rows:

TypeNameValue
MXsendfeedback-smtp.us-east-1.amazonses.com
TXTsendv=spf1 include:amazonses.com ~all
TXTresend._domainkeyp=MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3DQ... (a long string)

Your actual values will be different. Never copy the ones above; use the ones on your own screen.

This is the step to hand over

Take a screenshot of that table, or use the Share option if Resend offers one, and send it to Angelo or Robert. They will add the records and confirm when it is verified.

If you would rather do it yourself, you will need the login for wherever the domain is managed. Add each row exactly as shown, changing nothing. Then return to the Resend page and press Verify DNS Records. It usually goes green within fifteen minutes, though it can take a few hours.

One genuine risk, and how it is avoided

If a domain already sends email, it may already have a line of the v=spf1 kind. A domain is only allowed one, and adding a second quietly breaks email delivery for everybody. This is the single most common way this goes wrong. Using the send. prefix in step 2 avoids it entirely, because the new records sit on their own name and cannot collide with anything you already have. It is also why we would rather do this part for you.

Step 4: Create a key and paste it into the website

3Create the key

https://resend.com/api-keys

Click Create API Key. Give it a name you will recognise later, such as Website forms. For permission choose Sending access, which lets it send email and nothing else.

Resend shows you the key once. Copy it immediately. It begins with re_. If you lose it you cannot look it up again, but you can always delete it and make a new one, so this is an inconvenience rather than a disaster.

4Paste it into the website

In the editor, open Site Settings and choose the Forms tab. Fill in:

Resend API key Paste the key. Press Save. It is stored scrambled, and afterwards you will only ever see the first few characters followed by asterisks. That is expected.
Send notifications from An address on the domain you verified, for example website@send.thedesignboutique.com. It does not need to be a real inbox that anybody reads. The part after the @ must match what you verified in step 2, or Resend will refuse to send.
Sender name What appears in the inbox beside the address, such as The Design Boutique.
Replies go to Optional. If somebody hits reply on a notification, this is where it goes. Set it to a real inbox you read, otherwise replies go nowhere.

Press Publish to save the settings.

Step 5: Check it worked

  1. Open any form in Forms by Angelo and go to its Notifications tab. If the orange “Emails are not being sent yet” panel has gone, the website is satisfied.
  2. Make sure your own address is listed under Email these people.
  3. Go to the live contact page and submit the form yourself, as a visitor would.
  4. The email should arrive within a minute. Check the junk folder if it does not appear.

If something does not work

The orange panel is still there Either the key or the sending address is missing. The panel says which. Check you pressed Publish in Site Settings.
Nothing arrives, and the submission mentions the domain The domain is not verified yet, or the address after the @ does not match the verified domain. Go back to the Resend domains page and check it shows as verified.
Nothing arrives, and the submission mentions the key The key was deleted or regenerated inside Resend. Create a new one and paste it in again.
The email arrives in junk Mark it as not junk once. If it keeps happening, tell Angelo or Robert; a further record can be added to improve it.
Nothing arrives and nothing explains why Check Form Submissions in the editor. If the submission is there, the form worked and only the email failed, so no enquiry has been lost.
Where to see what Resend has sent

Resend keeps a log of every email, whether it was delivered, and whether it bounced. https://resend.com/emails
