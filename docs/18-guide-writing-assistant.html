The Writing Assistant

Drafting titles and descriptions  |  The Design Boutique  |  July 2026

Every page needs two short pieces of writing that almost nobody enjoys producing: the title and the description that appear in Google. They are the only part of a page most people ever read, they have to fit inside a character limit, and they have to be written again for every page on the site.

The assistant drafts them for you, from the content already on the page. You keep or discard what it gives you. It never writes anything on its own.

It does five jobs in total, all of them the kind of writing nobody looks forward to:

A search titleThe headline in Google. On the AI Writing Assistant tab.
A descriptionThe sentence under it. On the AI Writing Assistant tab.
Image descriptionsAlternative text, written by actually looking at the picture. On the image itself.
A question and answer sectionFour questions a customer would ask, added to the page as a proper FAQ. On the AI Writing Assistant tab.
A shorter paragraphRewrites your longest paragraph to say the same thing in fewer words. On the AI Writing Assistant tab.
Where: open any page, post, case study or event and click the AI Writing Assistant tab, and look for Suggest a title or description.

The tab is always there, whether or not the assistant is switched on. If it is off, the tab explains what it does and lists the steps to turn it on, so nothing is hidden and there is nothing to hunt for. The one exception is image descriptions, which stay on the image itself, since that is where you are when you need one.

Using it

1Press Suggest a title, or Suggest a description

It reads the page you are on: the heading, the body copy, and the focus keyword if you have set one. It takes a few seconds.

2Read what it gives you

The draft appears in a highlighted box with its character count, so you can see at a glance whether it fits. Nothing has been saved at this point. If you do not like it, press Suggest again and it will write a different one.

3Press Use this, or Discard

Use this puts the text into the field. Discard throws it away. Nothing is written to the page until you press Use this: the assistant cannot change your copy on its own.

Watch the Google preview while you do it

Further up the same tab is a preview of how the page looks as a search result. Accept a suggestion and it redraws immediately, so you can see the real thing rather than a character count. That is the fastest way to judge whether a description is doing its job.

Accepting is not publishing

A suggestion you accept goes into the draft, the same as any other edit. The orange dot appears and the live site is unchanged until you press Publish. You can try this on a real page and undo it by simply not publishing.

Describing an image

Every image needs a sentence describing it, for people using a screen reader and for search engines, which cannot see pictures either. Writing those by hand is the job that never gets done: this site currently has 78 images with none.

Where: click any image, and under the Alternative text box you will find Describe this image.

This is the one suggestion that is not drafted from the page's words. The picture itself is sent and looked at, so what comes back describes what is actually in it. It writes straight into the box, because there is nothing to weigh up: read it, and change it if it is wrong.

Check it, especially on photographs of people

It describes what it sees and it does not know your clients. If a picture is of a named person, a specific building or a particular product, it will describe them generically and you should put the name in. It is a first draft, not a caption service.

Drafting a question and answer section

A FAQ is the single most useful thing you can add to a page for AI assistants, because a question with a short answer under it is exactly the shape they quote. It is also the hardest thing to start, since it means inventing four questions from nothing.

Where: the AI Writing Assistant tab, under "Draft something for this page". Press Draft questions.
  1. It writes four questions and answers from what the page already says.
  2. Read them. They are phrased the way a customer would ask, not the way the business would.
  3. Press Add to the page and they appear at the bottom as an ordinary FAQ section, which you can then edit, reorder or move like any other block.
It will not invent facts, which means it sometimes gives you fewer

It is told never to make up prices, timescales, guarantees or credentials. If a page does not say enough to answer a question honestly, that question is left out. Getting two questions back rather than four is the tool behaving correctly, and it is telling you the page is thin.

Shortening a paragraph

On the AI Writing Assistant tab, Shorten the longest paragraph takes the longest block of text on the page and rewrites it to say the same thing in fewer words. It shows you both versions side by side with their lengths.

This one does not write into the page. It gives you a Copy button and you paste it over the paragraph yourself on the Edit tab. That is deliberate: your body copy is the part of the site that is genuinely yours, and nothing should rewrite it without you moving it across by hand.

What it is good at, and what it is not

Good at Fitting a lot of meaning into 60 or 160 characters, which is the tedious part. Finding the one sentence that describes a page you have been staring at for an hour. Getting you off a blank field.
Not good at Knowing anything the page does not say. It reads the page and nothing else: no access to your prices, your positioning, or what you meant to write but did not.
A thin page gives a thin suggestion

If a page has thirty words on it, the assistant has thirty words to work from and the result will be vague. That is not a fault in the tool, it is the tool telling you something true about the page. Where this happens, the answer is usually to write more on the page rather than to press Suggest again.

Treat what it produces as a first draft from somebody who has read the page carefully and has never met your business. That is genuinely what it is. Most suggestions are better with one word changed.

Turning it on and off

The assistant is off unless somebody switches it on, because unlike everything else in the editor it costs money per use. It needs an API key from an AI provider, and whoever owns that key pays for what it generates.

Where the settings liveSite Settings → Writing assistant
What it costsRoughly half a penny per suggestion. The settings screen shows an estimate for the model you pick, and updates if you change it.
How the key is storedEncrypted. After saving you will only ever see the first few characters followed by asterisks. That is expected: it cannot be read back out, only replaced.
Turning it offSwitch off Turn the Suggest button on. The button disappears from every page and nothing can be charged.
If the Suggest panel is not there at all

That means no key is configured, which is the normal state until somebody adds one. The rest of the SEO tab, the score, the checks, the readability notes and the Google preview, all work without it and cost nothing.

Questions you might have

Will it change my writing without asking?

No. It can only fill the title and description fields, and only when you press Use this.

Can I use it on every page at once?

No, deliberately. It works one page at a time so that somebody reads each suggestion before it is used. A hundred descriptions written and accepted unread would be worse than none.

Does it write blog posts?

No. It writes the two short fields that appear in Google, nothing else. The body of a page is yours.

Is what I write sent anywhere?

The content of the page you are on is sent to the AI provider so it has something to summarise, at the moment you press Suggest and not before. Nothing is sent while you type, and nothing is sent from pages you are not working on.

The assistant is optional. Every other part of the SEO tab works without it and always will. Correct as of July 2026.
