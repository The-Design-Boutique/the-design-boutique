Forms

How they work and how to build one  |  The Design Boutique  |  July 2026

Forms are built in the editor rather than in code. You create a form once, choose which questions it asks, and then place it on as many pages as you like. Change a question and it changes everywhere the form appears.

Where: Forms by Angelo in the left-hand list, for building them. To put one on a page, add a Form block to that page.
The contact form already works this way

Every contact form across the site now uses one shared form called Contact. If you want to add a question to the contact form, change it in that one place and it updates on every page at once.

Building a form

A form has four tabs. Most of the time you will only use the first.

QuestionsWhat the form asks, in order. Drag to reorder.
SettingsButton wording, and what happens after somebody submits.
NotificationsWho gets emailed, and anywhere else the answers should be sent.
Step routingOnly for long forms split into steps. Usually empty.

Question types

Add a question and pick its type. The useful ones:

Text, Long textA single line, or a bigger box for a message.
Email, PhoneSame as text, but checks what was typed looks right before allowing submission.
Dropdown, Radio buttons, CheckboxesA fixed set of choices. Dropdown and radio pick one; checkboxes pick any number.
Name, AddressSeveral boxes grouped together, so you do not have to build them one at a time.
ZIP codeFills in the city and state automatically once a valid ZIP is typed.
ConsentA tick box with legal wording beside it, for permission to contact somebody.
Section heading, Text blockNot questions. They break a long form up or explain something.
Page breakEverything after it appears on the next step. Use it to turn one long form into a few short ones.
Hidden fieldNever shown to the visitor. Records where the enquiry came from, such as which advert brought them.

Settings on every question

RequiredThe form cannot be submitted without an answer.
WidthFull, half or a third, so two short questions can sit side by side.
Help textA small line under the box explaining what you want.
Only show whenHides the question until another answer matches. See below.
Questions that appear only when relevant

Under any question is Only show when this field. Point it at another question, choose is, and type the answer that should reveal it.

For example, ask “Do you already have a website?” and set a follow up question “What is the address?” to show only when that answer is Yes. Somebody answering No never sees it, and is never asked to fill it in.

What happens when somebody submits

Every submission is saved in the editor under Form Submissions, whether or not anybody is emailed. That is the record, and it cannot be lost by an email going astray.

Under Notifications you can add email addresses to be told about each one. Leave it empty and submissions are still saved, just silently.

Under Settings you choose what the visitor sees afterwards: a thank you message, or being sent to another page such as the Thank You page.

Email needs to be switched on once, for the whole site

A website cannot send email by itself. It has to hand each message to a service that does, and that service has to be told the site is allowed to send using your domain name. Until that is set up, the addresses under Notifications are just a list: nobody is emailed.

This is a one time job, covered in the separate Setting Up Email guide. If it has not been done, the Notifications tab tells you so in an orange panel at the top. Nothing is lost in the meantime, because every submission is still saved.

Sending answers to another system

Most sites never need this section. Emailing the enquiry, and having it saved in the editor, is enough. Skip this unless somebody has specifically asked you to connect the forms to another piece of software.

Under Notifications, below the email addresses, is Send to another system. It does exactly what it says: as well as emailing people, every submission can be handed straight to another program the moment it arrives, without anyone copying and pasting it.

What this is actually for

Three situations come up in practice:

A CRM Software that keeps a record of every customer and enquiry, such as HubSpot, Salesforce or Zoho. Connecting the form means a new enquiry becomes a new contact in the CRM automatically, already filled in, rather than somebody retyping it from an email.
An automation tool Zapier and Make are services that sit between programs and pass information along. You tell Zapier “when the website form is submitted, add a row to this spreadsheet, then send a text message to the office”. They are useful precisely because they connect to thousands of other programs, so you are not limited to what this website knows how to talk to.
A mailing list Adding somebody to Mailchimp or a similar newsletter tool the moment they tick the consent box, instead of exporting a list once a month.

The word “webhook”

You will meet this word as soon as you set one of these up, and it sounds far more technical than it is. A webhook is simply a private web address that a program gives you so other programs can send it information. Think of it as a post box: the receiving program hands you the address of its post box, and anything you post there arrives instantly inside that program.

You never have to build one or understand how it works. The receiving program creates it and shows it to you. Your only job is to copy it and paste it into the Web address to send to box. In Zapier, for instance, you create a new Zap, choose “Webhooks” as the trigger, pick “Catch Hook”, and Zapier displays an address for you to copy.

Treat that address like a password

Anybody who has the address can send information into your CRM or spreadsheet. It is not secret in the way a password is, but it should not be posted publicly or shared more widely than necessary. If one is ever misused, the receiving program can issue a new address and you paste the new one in.

What actually gets sent

Every time somebody submits the form, the answers are bundled up and posted to that address. The bundle is sometimes called the payload, which is just a name for “the information being delivered”. It contains the answers to the questions, plus which form it came from and the date and time.

Renaming fields, and when you need to

The questions arrive under the names they have on the form. Usually that is fine: Zapier and most CRMs will show you the list and let you decide where each one goes at their end.

Occasionally the receiving system is fussy and insists on its own names. It might refuse anything that is not called email_address, when your question is called “Your email”. That is what Rename fields for this system is for: on the left the name as it appears on your form, on the right the name that system demands. If nobody has told you a system needs this, leave it empty.

The remaining two settings

Method Leave as POST. Almost every system wants POST, and the one that does not will tell you so explicitly.
Send every field On by default, which sends the whole submission and is normally what you want. Turn it off to send only the fields you renamed above. That is worth doing when the form asks something the other system has no business storing, such as a free text message containing personal details.
Adding one does not switch anything else off

Connecting another system is additional. Emails still go out, submissions are still saved in the editor, and you can add several destinations at once. If the other system is down or the address is wrong, the submission is still saved and the emails are still sent: the handover simply does not happen, and nothing is lost.

Putting a form on a page

  1. Open the page and add a Form block where you want it.
  2. Choose which form.
  3. Pick a style: two columns with an introduction beside it, centred, or plain.

The two-column style is the one used across the site today, with the heading, bullet points and address on the left and the form on the right.

Questions you might have

I changed the contact form. Do I need to update every page?

No. Every page using that form updates at once.

Can I have two different forms?

Yes. Create as many as you like and choose which one each Form block uses. A short newsletter signup and a long enquiry form can live side by side.

Where do the answers go?

Form Submissions in the editor, always. Email notifications are optional and additional.

Somebody said the form did not work.

Check Form Submissions first. If their answer is there, the form worked and the problem is with the notification email. If it is not there, note what they were trying to do and pass it on.

Nobody is getting the notification emails.

Open the form and look at the Notifications tab. If email has not been set up for the site, an orange panel at the top says so and tells you what is missing. See the Setting Up Email guide. The submissions themselves are safe either way.

Do I need Zapier or a CRM?

No. They are entirely optional. The site emails you and keeps every submission on its own.

Submissions are validated both in the visitor’s browser and again on the server, so a form cannot be bypassed. Correct as of July 2026.
