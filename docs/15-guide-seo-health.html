SEO Health

The checks beside every page, and what to do about them  |  The Design Boutique  |  July 2026

Open any page, post, case study or event and you will see four tabs across the top: Edit, SEO, Search and AEO. Edit is the page itself. The other three answer three different questions about it.

SEOIs this page well built? A checklist that updates as you type. Everything here is under your control right now.
SearchHow is this page doing in Google? Real figures, updated once a day. Nothing you type changes these today.
AEOCould an AI assistant quote this page? A different kind of readiness, explained on its own below.
Nothing here costs anything or waits on anything

The SEO and AEO checks are worked out from the page in front of you, instantly, with no charge. The Search figures are collected once a night and stored, so opening the tab never waits for Google.

The SEO tab

A score out of 100 at the top, then the checks grouped so the ones needing attention come first. Each says what is wrong and what to do, and each is worth a stated number of points so you can see which are worth your time.

A score in the seventies is normal and often fine

This is not an exam. Some checks do not apply to some pages, and those are left out of the score rather than counted against it: the panel says how many points were set aside and why. Chasing 100 on a page that does not need it is wasted effort.

How this looks in Google

Below the checks is a preview of the page as a search result, which updates as you type the title and description. It shows both the desktop and the phone version, because Google cuts them off at different points.

It is a close approximation rather than a promise. Google rewrites the title on well over half of all results and writes its own description whenever it thinks another sentence answers the search better. What you write is the strongest signal you can give it, not an instruction.

Technical

Results from Google's own page test, refreshed nightly. These are about how the page is built rather than what it says, so most of them are not things an editor can fix, and they are listed so you know they were checked rather than so you act on them.

The Search tab

What Google reports about this page over the last 28 days. The important thing to understand is that these figures describe the live thedesignboutique.com, not this preview. The preview is deliberately hidden from Google, so it has no search presence of its own.

ClicksHow many people came to the page from a search.
ImpressionsHow many times it appeared in results, whether or not anybody clicked.

Lots of impressions and no clicks is a message

It means Google is offering the page to people and they are choosing something else. That is usually the title and description doing their job badly, which is the one thing on this tab you can fix directly, on the SEO tab, and watch in the preview while you do it.

What people searched to find this page

The top five search terms, each with the position this page holds for it. These will not add up to the totals above: Google keeps rare searches private, counting them in the totals but never saying what was typed.

When a page is not in Google

The tab explains what to do, and it is worth knowing why it cannot do it for you. Google publishes no way for other software to request that a page be indexed. The button in Google's own tool has no equivalent that another program can press. So the tab gives you a link straight to Google's screen for that exact page, and the three presses to make once you are there.

There is also a button to notify the other search engines, Bing, Yandex and several more, which is instant and automatic. It does not include Google, which has never taken part in that system. Until the new site is the live site, that button is switched off and says why.

The AEO tab

People increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI answers for a recommendation rather than scrolling a list of links. Those assistants read pages and quote from them, and a page that is easy to quote gets used more often than one that is not.

This measures how quotable a page is, not whether it was quoted

Google publishes the figures on the Search tab, which is why those are real. No AI assistant publishes anything equivalent: there is no way for anyone to know whether ChatGPT cited a page.

So this score is about readiness, and it is worth being blunt that any product claiming to report your AI visibility is estimating it. What is being measured here is the part you can actually control.

Seven things, roughly in order of how much they matter:

Answers up frontThe opening paragraph is the part that gets pulled out and quoted. A greeting occupies the place where the answer should be.
Headings match how people askPeople ask assistants things like “how much does branding cost”. A heading in those words is what gets matched.
Has a question and answer sectionA FAQ is the single most quotable thing a page can carry.
Broken into sectionsSo a section can be lifted out whole and still make sense.
Enough to be worth quotingThin pages lose to fuller ones answering the same question.
Says who wrote it and whenBlog posts only. Assistants weigh authorship and recency.
Describes itself specificallyChoosing Service or FAQPage on the SEO tab says more than the default.

Where to start on a page that scores badly

  1. Fix anything under Worth fixing on the SEO tab. That list is already in order.
  2. Write a real title and description, watching the preview as you type.
  3. If the Search tab shows impressions but almost no clicks, rewrite the description. It is being seen and passed over.
  4. On the AEO tab, add a short FAQ. It is usually the largest single gain available.
