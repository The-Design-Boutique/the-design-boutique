Editing Your Website

A guide for anyone coming from WordPress  |  The Design Boutique  |  July 2026

Your website now runs on two pieces: an editor where the words and pictures live, and the website itself, which reads from it. You will only ever use the editor. It does the same job as the WordPress admin, and this guide is written on the assumption that WordPress is what you know.

The differences worth knowing on day one

There are no plugins Nothing to update, nothing that can break the site overnight, and no security patches to chase. Everything the old site used plugins for is built in.
Nothing is live until you publish WordPress saves drafts, but its editor and its live page are the same thing. Here, your changes are saved constantly and privately as you work. The public site does not change until you press Publish.
Pages are built from blocks Similar to Elementor, but the blocks are the ones your site actually uses, so there is a much shorter list and none of them can be arranged into something broken.
You cannot break the design Fonts, colours, spacing and layout are set once, in the site itself. There is no styling panel because there is nothing to style. This is deliberate: it is why the site cannot drift out of shape over time.
Two people can edit at once You will see their cursor. Nobody overwrites anybody.

Editing a page

  1. Open Pages and click the one you want.
  2. Change what you need. It saves as you type; there is no Save button and nothing to lose.
  3. Press View page to see it in a new tab exactly as it will look, including changes nobody else can see yet.
  4. When you are happy, press Publish.
The orange dot

A page with unpublished changes shows an orange dot. It means the version you are looking at is not the version the public sees. Publishing clears it.

Working with blocks

Below the page title is the page itself, built from blocks stacked in order. Drag the handle at the left of a block to move it. Use the menu at its right to duplicate or remove it. The plus button between blocks adds a new one.

Most blocks have a Section settings group for the background colour and spacing. Those options are the ones your design allows, which is why there are only a few.

Images

Drag a picture in, or click to choose one. Two things are worth doing every time:

  • Alternative text. One sentence describing the picture. It is read aloud to people who cannot see it, and it is the only thing Google can understand about an image.
  • The crop. Click the crop icon and set the focal point on the part that matters. The site crops images differently on a phone, and this tells it what to keep.

You do not need to resize or compress anything. Upload the original and the site produces the right size and format for each visitor automatically.

Undoing something

Every change is kept. The clock icon at the top right opens the history: scroll back, see who changed what, and restore any earlier version. Nothing is ever really lost, including a deleted page.

Writing a blog post

  1. Open Laney Said (Blog) and press the plus.
  2. Give it a title. The web address fills itself in; you can change it before publishing.
  3. Set the author, the date, a category and a featured image.
  4. Write the post.
  5. Check the SEO tab, then publish.
Changing the address of a page that is already live

If you change the address of something already published, the editor will offer to leave a forwarding note behind so old links still work. Say yes. Links live in other people’s emails, other websites and Google’s index, and none of those update when you rename a page.

The four tabs on every page

Beside Edit you will find SEO, Search and AEO. They are covered properly in the separate SEO Health guide. In short: SEO is a checklist you can act on now, Search is how the page is really doing in Google, and AEO is whether an AI assistant could quote it.

Things you might reasonably worry about

Can I break the site?

Not visually, and not permanently. You cannot change the design, and every version is kept and restorable. The worst you can do is publish something you would rather not have, and that can be put back in a few clicks.

Where do form enquiries go?

Always into Form Submissions, whether or not an email is sent. If somebody says they never heard back, look there first: if their message is in the list, the form worked and only the email failed.

Somebody sent me a link that gives an error

Open Dead Links. If the address is listed, type where it should go and press the button. That is the whole job.

Do I need to do anything for SEO?

Write a real title and description on every new page, and give every image alternative text. Those two habits are most of it. The SEO tab tells you the rest, page by page.

Who do I ask?

The written guides cover forms, email setup, SEO health, site speed and the search files. Anything they do not answer is worth asking rather than guessing at.
