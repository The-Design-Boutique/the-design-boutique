How to read the “short” findings

A word count is a blunt measure and it is wrong about some pages on purpose. The Work page, the Trusted page and the blog index are lists of other things; they are short because that is what they are, and lengthening them would make them worse. The same is true of a case study built around photographs.

The reason it is still worth listing is that search engines and AI assistants have no way of telling a deliberately visual page from a page somebody never finished. Both look identical from the outside: not much to read. Where a page is short by design, the fix is usually a paragraph of context rather than an essay.

Suggested order

  1. The ADA Compliance page. Real wording instead of filler. This is the only item that is unambiguously a defect.
  2. Alternative text on images. A sentence per picture. It helps people using a screen reader and it helps Google, and it changes nothing visually.
  3. The four missing descriptions. A sentence each.
  4. A summary under each video post. Turns thirteen invisible posts into thirteen findable ones.
  5. Case studies. The largest opportunity and the largest amount of work. Worth treating as its own project rather than a tidy-up.
A note on what this check does not cover

This looked for missing and placeholder content, not for quality. Spelling, tone, accuracy and whether each page still says what the business wants it to say in 2026 have not been reviewed. A proper read-through by somebody who knows the business would find things no automated check can. Two small examples already spotted by eye: the Thank You page reads “more revenue than you could have ever imaged” and “we will get back to you licky split”. Both are inherited from the existing site.
